# Question screening (question set v2)

Generated by `anchoring_llms.select_items`.

Each candidate question was asked without an anchor, 20 times per thinking level (off, low). An anchoring question is only useful if the model does **not** know the answer: in pilot run 2, Qwen3-32B knew 13 of the 15 questions, so the anchors had nothing to pull. This screening keeps the questions the model is unsure about.

## How to read the numbers

- **known**: share of answers within 2% of the true value (the highest share over the thinking levels). High means the model knows the number.
- **middle 80%**: the 10th to the 90th percentile of the answers. A wide range means the model is unsure.
- **spread** (Study 1): log10 of (90th percentile / 10th percentile), averaged over levels. 0 = the same answer every time, 0.3 = the high answers are twice the low ones, 1 = ten times.
- **adjustment** (Study 2): (median − R) / (true value − R), where R is the value a solver starts from (365 days, 9.81 m/s², 100 °C, ...). 0 = the answer stayed at R, 1 = it reached the true value.
- **parsed**: share of answers that contained a number.

## Rules

Question rules are checked on **qwen3-32b**, the largest screened model: a question it does not know, a smaller model does not know either. The number rule is checked on every screened model.

- every model: at least 75% of answers contain a number (at every level; models other than qwen3-32b only with thinking low)
- qwen3-32b: at most 50% of answers within 2% of the truth (Study 1: at any thinking level; Study 2: with thinking off)
- Study 1: spread (averaged over levels) at least 0.1 and at most 2.0; median no more than 10 times off the truth (else the question may be unclear)
- Study 2: adjustment with thinking off between 0.0 and 0.8; with thinking low the median gets closer to the truth by at least 0.2 (in adjustment units, so thinking has something to work with)
- Study 1: at most 3 questions per topic, 15 questions; Study 2: at most 15 per topic, 10 questions
- ranking: Study 1 by spread (most unsure first); Study 2 by how much closer thinking brings the answer (largest first)

The middle 80% of its answers ran from 1,290 to 14,100, with a median of 1,350. None of its answers were within 2% of the truth (at any thinking level), so the model does not know this number. Its spread is 1.04: the high answers are about 10.8 times the low ones. An anchor has room to pull here.

The middle 80% of its answers ran from 366.3 to 433, with a median of 367. Only 30% of its answers were within 2% of the truth (with thinking off), so the model does not know this number without thinking. Starting from R = 365, its median moved 3% of the way to the true value (adjustment 0.03), so there is room for more thinking to adjust further. With thinking low, its median was 433 (adjustment 0.95).
